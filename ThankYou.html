Stop — this was a phishing simulation

If this were a real attack, you would have just handed your account details to a stranger. This page is part of a security-awareness exercise.

✓ Nothing you typed was saved. Your name, username, and password were discarded in your browser and never sent anywhere — not even hashed.

What gave it away

  • Free reward for credentials. Legitimate sites don't lock ordinary articles behind a sudden "create an account with your password" wall.
  • Urgency & curiosity. An interesting headline pushed you to act before thinking. That's the lure.
  • Check the address bar. Real registration happens on the site's own verified domain, over HTTPS — not a look-alike page.
  • Password reuse is the real damage. If the password you entered is one you use elsewhere, an attacker would now try it on your email and bank.
  • Unexpected fields. Being asked for full legal name and a password just to read a news post is a red flag.

What to do when you spot one

  • Don't enter anything. Close the tab.
  • Report it to your IT/security team or the organization being impersonated.
  • If you already entered a password, change it everywhere you used it and enable MFA.
